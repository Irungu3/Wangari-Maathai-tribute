Professor Wangari Maathai

Founder of the Green Belt Movement & Nobel Peace Prize Laureate

Professor Wangari Maathai, whose environmental and social activism sparked a global movement.

Timeline of an Extraordinary Life

  • 1940 - Born in Nyeri, Kenya.
  • 1964 - Earned a degree in Biology from Mount St. Scholastica College in Kansas, becoming part of the Kennedy Airlift program.
  • 1971 - Completed her Ph.D. at the University of Nairobi, making history as the first woman from East and Central Africa to earn a doctorate.
  • 1977 - Founded the Green Belt Movement, an grassroots organization focused on environmental conservation and women's rights through planting trees.
  • 2002 - Elected to the Kenyan Parliament with an overwhelming 98% of the vote, serving as Assistant Minister for Environment and Natural Resources.
  • 2004 - Awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for her holistic approach to sustainable development, democracy, and peace. She was the first African woman to receive this honor.
  • 2011 - Passed away in Nairobi, leaving a legacy of over 51 million trees planted and a powerful blueprint for environmental activism worldwide.

Vision & Legacy

blockquote> "When we plant trees, we plant the seeds of peace and seeds of hope."

— Wangari Maathai

Professor Maathai connected environmental degradation directly to poverty and political oppression. By empowering rural women to plant trees, she fought deforestation while simultaneously cultivating civic education, democratic voice, and economic independence.
